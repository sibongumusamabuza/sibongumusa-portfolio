About

Bringing systems and skills together, for people who need both

My work has moved between two things that usually stay separate: building the systems behind digital learning, and standing in front of a room teaching people to use them. At Emalalatini Development Centre I built student management systems and helped pilot a Moodle learning platform across schools in Eswatini. At ESCCOM, through the Universal Access Service Fund, I took that same platform-building instinct into the classroom — facilitating hands-on digital skills training for out-of-school youth and adults, both online and in person.

Today, I continue that work independently — providing tutoring and teaching to people who want to build their digital skills, one-on-one or in small groups.

My Professional Journey

Two chapters, one thread: technology that people can actually use

My professional journey has brought together information technology, systems development, digital learning, and community digital skills training.

01

Emalalatini Development Centre College

My first work experience was an internship at Emalalatini Development Centre College — after the internship, I was kept on under contract, working across IT, systems development, and marketing while supporting digital learning initiatives.

I built a Student Registration System and a Student Performance Management System to support student administration and academic performance tracking, and was also responsible for registering students directly.

Alongside the systems work, I handled day-to-day administration for the department — emails and correspondence, phoning students directly about registration and enquiries, and keeping records up to date.

As part of piloting the Moodle digital learning platform across roughly 65 schools around Eswatini, I visited schools directly to orient both students and teachers on the system — introducing students to ICT for the first time, and helping students and lecturers troubleshoot technical issues along the way.

02

ESCCOM — Universal Access Service Fund

After Emalalatini, I joined the Eswatini Communications Commission (ESCCOM) through the Universal Access Service Fund (UASF) as a Digital Skills Facilitator on the National Digital Skills Programme, delivering the "Connecting the Unconnected" project at the Ezulwini Community ICT Centre — run in partnership with the MTN Eswatini Foundation, UNDP, SEDCO, ENYC, and Wanderport Eswatini.

I trained out-of-school youth and adults, in person and online, building confidence with computers, Microsoft 365, Google Workspace, Gmail, Canva, cybersecurity, data protection, and other career-related digital tools — alongside learner administration: attendance, timetables, performance reporting, certificates, and day-to-day correspondence — emailing and calling learners directly to follow up on enquiries and attendance.

Facilitating both physical and online lessons meant I was as much a tutor as a facilitator — walking learners through concepts one-on-one as often as leading a full room.

My ESCCOM/UASF contract concluded in February 2026, as the program's pilot wrapped its final cohort.

What connects both chapters

From developing digital learning systems and supporting Moodle implementation in schools, to facilitating practical computer skills within communities — the thread running through both roles has been connecting technology, learning, and people.

I'm drawn to work that uses technology not just to build systems, but to open up access to learning and strengthen digital confidence — so that no learner is left behind.

Systems Development

Student Registration System

Built with Microsoft Excel & Google Forms

Emalalatini Development Centre College needed a faster, more reliable way to take in new students than paper forms and manual filing. I designed a Google Form as the front-end intake point, feeding into a structured Excel workbook that organised, sorted, and verified each student's registration details.

I also registered students directly at the front line, so the system was shaped by what actually slowed the process down — and built to fix it.

Student Performance Management System

Built with Java & HTML

To help staff track academic performance without digging through paper report cards, I developed a Student Performance Management System using Java for the underlying logic and HTML for the interface — giving the department a straightforward way to record, view, and monitor how students were performing over time.

It sits alongside the Registration System as part of the same push: replace manual record-keeping with tools staff could actually rely on.

Digital Learning & School Support

Moodle Learning Platform Pilot

Moodle LMS · On-site training & support

I helped pilot Moodle as a digital learning platform across roughly 65 schools in Eswatini — for many students and teachers, their first real introduction to ICT. That meant visiting schools in person to set up and orient both groups on the system, then staying hands-on to troubleshoot the technical issues that came up along the way.

I later helped co-build "Information and Communication Technology (5924)" — an interactive Moodle course for the Eswatini Prevocational Certificate of Secondary Education, built directly on top of that pilot work.
